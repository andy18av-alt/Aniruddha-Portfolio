The idea

Retailers lose money and customer trust to fake listings, fraudulent sellers, abusive returns and insider theft. Today most of it is caught late, by people working queues. I propose one Trust and Safety suite that checks every listing, order, payout and claim as it happens, and acts before the loss.

The contextRegulators now fine listing by listing, abuse is organised (one device, thousands of orders), and manual review cannot keep up.
Who it is forMarketplaces and large retailers, and the brands that sell on them.
How it helpsLess fraud and fewer bad returns, honest sellers and customers left alone, and evidence ready for any regulator or appeal.

How the suite works, in six steps

Three checks run on every event. Their answers feed one decision. The suite then acts and learns.

Check 1 · partnerWhoIs this person real, and linked to anyone bad?e.g. a new seller shares a device and bank account with a banned one
Check 2 · buildWhatDoes it match the real product and the rules?e.g. a listing shows a brand logo with no brand authorisation
Check 3 · buildWhenIs the pattern or timing normal?e.g. orders jump 10x and a payout is requested on day 3
ThenDecideOne risk score, applied through written rulesevery rule versioned, so decisions are explainable
ThenActRespond in proportion to the riskallow, add friction, hold, or block; people take the grey middle
ThenLearnLog the reason, learn from outcomesappeals and results improve the next decision

Highlights from my Flipkart work

A few relevant examples, not the full scope.

  • Customer abuse prevention: automated checks and signals that routed high-risk transactions to fraud investigation.
  • Seller protection fund claims: rules plus an investigation queue comparing doorstep pickup photos with seller claim photos, against patterns like one broken unit shot from many angles.
  • Listing go-live checks: brand authorisation and trademark certificates for gated brands, FSSAI licences for food; aiming for OCR so routine checks never reach a person.
  • Reseller abuse: a device-intelligence partnership that linked accounts, devices and bank accounts to flag risky ones.
  • Selected results: owned written adjudication frameworks and policy design for fraud and abuse programs; real-time adjudication for about 400,000 customers a month at a 2.5-minute SLA; policy deployment cut from 7 days to 24 hours; counterfeit reduced by about 5 bps.

Five design principles

  1. Prevent before you police: catch at listing, onboarding and payout.
  2. Score, then act in proportion: automate clear cases, people for the middle, hold the severe.
  3. Measure the cost of mistakes: wrongly blocked sellers and customers are losses too.
  4. Keep evidence: every decision records its reason, for appeal, audit and regulator.
  5. Fix the root cause: fewer escalations, not faster replies.

Trust breaks in six places, and most fixes are still manual

A retailer loses trust when the product is not what was promised, never arrives, is fake, or when the process after the order feels unfair. Most of these are handled with review queues, blunt rules and people.

Build the suite, partner for parts

The retailer buys one suite. Inside it, the brain and the "what" layer are built; identity and device signals are plugged in from partners first, because those markets are crowded and need a data network across many clients.

Phase 1 · Year 1

Build the "what" layer, decision engine, case management, policy and audit trail. Plug in device, KYC and payment risk through partners (Bureau, SHIELD, IDfy and similar).

Phase 2 · Year 2

Add the "when" models (payout, returns, COD) and package by use case for retailers. One contract, one dashboard, one evidence trail.

Phase 3 · Optional

With enough retailers on the suite, shared signals (linked sellers, repeat abusers, catalog fingerprints) become a data network. Then decide which partner parts to build in-house.

Why not build everything on day one: identity and device intelligence need cross-client data and regulatory work (KYC, data protection) and would be slow to differentiate. Owning the decision and catalog truth is where the defensible value sits.

Operating model and metrics

Roles: policy owner, data and model owner, reviewer team with quality sampling, appeals owner. Cadence: weekly review of top root causes, monthly policy review.

DimensionMeasures
HarmViolation prevalence in the live catalog; fraud loss per ₹100 of payout; complaints per 1,000 orders
AccuracyPrecision and recall by rule; share caught before payout or before listing
Cost of mistakesHonest sellers wrongly held; customers wrongly refused; appeal overturn rate
SpeedTime from signal to action; time to sweep the catalog after a rule changes
EfficiencyCost per decision; automation rate; reviewer hours saved

How I would start: 90 days

Days 1–30 · Baseline

Measure losses by problem with Finance and rank by rupees. Pick the two or three with the biggest, clearest loss.

Days 31–60 · Pilot

For example, the seller payout-risk score with tiered holds, or a catalog check on two or three regulated categories. Shadow mode first, then a randomised half against a control.

Days 61–90 · Prove and scale

Compare against the control, publish the cost-of-mistakes metrics, and decide what to scale.

In plain words

If a ₹10,000 crore marketplace ran this suite, my estimate is that it would save or earn about ₹41–131 crore a year (0.4–1.3% of sales). The upper figure, the base case, breaks down like this:

  • ₹23 crLess fraud. Fraud of about 0.3% of sales is ₹30 crore; with investigation and recovery it really costs about 3x that (₹92 crore). Cutting it by a quarter saves about ₹23 crore.
  • ₹35 crFewer avoidable returns. About 2.5 crore returns a year; if 1 in 5 comes from a bad listing or abuse, and the suite stops 1 in 5 of those, that is 10 lakh fewer returns at about ₹350 each.
  • ₹29 crFewer refused COD orders. About 83 lakh COD orders come back unsold; stopping 1 in 10 at about ₹350 each.
  • ₹10 crMore sales from trusted listings. 0.5% more sales is ₹50 crore, of which the marketplace keeps about 20%.
  • ₹34 crLess manual review. 5 crore listing, review and claim decisions; automating 70% cuts the cost from about ₹10 to ₹0.20 each.

The low case, about ₹41 crore, uses cautious inputs on the same five levers. These are my assumptions, not data; in a real engagement the first 30 days replace them with the retailer's own numbers.
